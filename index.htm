Gold’s effect: 2000–2026

Research illustration only. These results are hypothetical, not personal advice, a recommendation or a forecast. You can lose money.

We tested whether adding gold to SPY or QQQ reduced large losses. Berkshire, gold and five individual stocks provide comparisons.

W2: January 3, 2000–October 5, 2026 · W1: January 4, 2021–October 5, 2026 · Generated 2026-11-04 · Dollar values are nominal, with reinvestment and no deposits or withdrawals.

Conclusion

Among the choices tested, 50% SPY / 50% gold looks least fragile for someone focused on avoiding large losses and reliance on one company. QQQ with gold also held up better than QQQ alone, but its losses were larger than SPY with gold in the long test.

The loss comparisonIn a poor W2 simulation outcome, SPY alone fell about 64% from a high; 50% SPY / 50% gold fell about 41%. QQQ alone fell about 80%; 50% QQQ / 50% gold fell about 49%.
Why a fund helpsSPY holds roughly 500 companies across industries. QQQ holds roughly 100 and is more concentrated in technology. Either spreads company risk more than owning one stock.
One standout is still one stockBerkshire with gold had a slightly smaller measured fall than SPY with gold, but Berkshire is a single company whose past success cannot identify a future winner.

A large fall matters: after a 40% loss, you need about a 67% gain just to get back to the old high. Gold reduced losses in these tests, but cannot guarantee protection in the future.

Why we use the longer test

Our main test, W2 (2000–26), includes the dot-com bust, the 2008 financial crisis, the 2020 pandemic shock and the 2022 decline. The shorter W1 test (2021–26) includes 2022, but none of those earlier events. W2 gives us more kinds of past trouble to test against. It does not make an investment safer or rule out a worse future. W1 overlaps W2; it is a recent check, not a second independent test. Before late 2004, the gold part uses an estimate because the gold ETF did not yet exist.

Reading the table: A drawdown is a fall from a previous high. P50 is the middle simulated result. P05 is a poor-outcome threshold: about 5% of simulated paths were worse, so it is not the worst possible loss. CAGR means annual compounded growth. SR compares growth with day-to-day ups and downs; higher is generally better. Δ compares a result with QQQ in the same period. Annual reset or buy once and leave it?

What we found: In the long W2 test, resetting once a year had higher middle growth for all nine stock/gold mixes. For the 50/50 mixes, it also had smaller poor-outcome losses. This does not tell us which method will win in the future.

Annual reset restores the chosen mix every January. No reset buys once and lets the mix drift. A calendar reset is not an attempt to predict market turns. The dollar amounts below repeat one growth rate every year; they are examples, not forecasts.

Method and limits

Market-asset closes are dividend-reinvested total-return histories. The gold series is a synthetic spot-based proxy before November 18, 2004, then gold ETF total return. The earlier portion was not tradable as that ETF and omits its expense. The mixes in the main MC table and growth charts rebalance at the first January trading close each year and hold shares between resets. The no-reset SPY/QQQ mixes buy once at the first close and then hold the original shares as the weights drift. Single assets remain buy and hold. Every market-asset trade costs 0.05% of the amount traded, including the first purchase. The backtest assumes exact closing-price sizing and fractional shares.

Each MC study resamples 21-trading-day blocks of already completed daily portfolio returns into 100,000 paths. Trading decisions are not recalculated inside simulated paths. Sharpe is calculated from average daily simple return divided by daily return standard deviation, multiplied by √252; historical results use sample standard deviation and MC uses population standard deviation. The risk-free rate is 0%. The portfolios were selected after looking at past results and have no live validation. Future returns, relationships and costs can differ.
